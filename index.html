Overview & Objective

Today I completed the simulation of a Buck (Step-Down) DC-DC Converter using LTspice as part of my power electronics learning journey.

The objective was to step down the input voltage using PWM control and observe the converter's transient and steady-state behavior.

Debugging & Learning

During the process, I encountered an unexpected output waveform. Instead of accepting the result, I investigated the circuit, checked the component models, verified the switching operation, and analyzed the MOSFET gate drive. Debugging the simulation helped me understand the importance of correct device models, switching topology, and transient analysis in power electronics.

Key Learnings

  • PWM-based voltage regulation
  • Buck converter operating principles
  • Transient response analysis in LTspice
  • Troubleshooting simulation models and circuit behavior
  • Importance of careful verification in circuit design

Every simulation issue turned into an opportunity to deepen my understanding of power electronics. Looking forward to exploring more converter topologies and control techniques.

Case Study Overview

🚀 Simulated Closed-Loop Speed Control of a DC Motor using a DC-DC Buck Converter in MATLAB/Simulink!

Designing and testing power electronics and drive systems virtually is one of the most effective ways to validate dynamic performance before hitting the lab.

System Design & Architecture

01
DC-DC Buck Converter

Regulates the variable DC voltage applied to the motor armature through Pulse Width Modulation (PWM).

02
Feedback Control Loop

Measures motor speed, compares it against a reference command, and passes the error through a tuned PI controller to dynamically update the PWM duty cycle.

03
Dynamic Performance

Evaluated system response under sudden reference speed changes and step torque load disturbances to ensure stability, fast settling time, and minimal overshoot.

04
Simulation Analysis

Simulink provided clear insights into voltage ripple, current dynamics, and speed regulation under varying operating conditions.

Smart Wireless Power Transfer System for Electric Vehicle Charging

Project Concept & Motivation

This project aims to develop a wireless charging system for electric vehicles (EVs) using inductive power transfer technology. The system transfers electrical energy from a transmitter coil installed in a charging pad to a receiver coil mounted underneath the electric vehicle, eliminating the need for physical charging cables.

When the vehicle is positioned over the charging pad, the transmitter generates a high-frequency alternating magnetic field. The receiver coil captures this magnetic energy and converts it into electrical energy, which is then rectified and regulated to charge the EV battery.

Proposed System Features

  • Automatic vehicle-position detection
  • Charging-status indication & visual telemetry
  • Real-time voltage & current monitoring
  • Integrated overcurrent & thermal protection
Prototype Concept

A small-scale prototype can be developed for the IDEA Lab to demonstrate contactless charging and measure the efficiency of power transfer at different distances and coil alignments.

Main Objective

To design and demonstrate a safe, efficient, and convenient wireless EV charging system that can reduce dependence on conventional plug-in charging.

Expected Outcome

A working prototype that demonstrates wireless transfer of electrical power to an EV battery and displays important parameters such as voltage, current, power, charging status, and efficiency.
